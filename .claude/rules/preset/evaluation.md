# Evaluation and comparison

A document that compares options:

- States its selection criteria up front and justifies each from the problem, not from the winner.
- Describes every option on its own terms, including the workloads where that option is the right
  choice, so a reader cannot infer the outcome from the descriptions alone.
- Labels criteria that are project-specific rather than universal, and acknowledges them as
  contestable.
- Keeps verdicts conditional on the stated criteria ("of the options meeting these criteria, ..."),
  never absolute ("falls short", "wins").
- Names what each option, including any chosen one, gives up.
- Marks every option that would revise an existing decision as such.
- Leaves the decision to the decision record. The comparison is the evidence, presented so a
  skeptic can disagree with a premise rather than be told the answer.

Before handing a proposal over, have a read-only reviewer check it for drift and for leaning toward
a verdict, and fix what it finds.
